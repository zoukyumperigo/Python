Chapter 1

What is route optimization?

Route optimization is figuring out the smartest, most efficient way for your drivers to complete their stops — not just the shortest path, but the best path given real-world constraints like traffic, delivery time windows, vehicle capacity, driver schedules, and customer priorities.

Modern route optimization software uses algorithms (and increasingly, AI) to analyze dozens of variables simultaneously and produce routes that no human dispatcher could match by hand in the same time frame.

Chapter 2

Can't I just optimize routes manually?

With 3 drivers and 15 stops, maybe. With 15 drivers and 300 stops? The number of possible route combinations becomes astronomically large — far beyond what any human can reasonably evaluate in the time available before the morning run.

✗
Human error

Missed time windows, overlooked capacity limits, and wrong turn-by-turn sequences are common in manual planning.

✗
Impossible to scale

Manual planning doesn't grow with your business. Each new driver or stop multiplies planning complexity.

✗
Knowledge dependency

Route knowledge locked in one dispatcher's head is a single point of failure.

✗
No real-time flexibility

When plans change mid-day, manual re-routing takes too long and introduces new errors.

Signs it's time to switch: increasing late deliveries · rising fuel costs · planning takes over 30 minutes · can't keep up with business growth

Frequently asked questions

What is a Route Optimization API?

A route optimization API accepts a list of stops and constraints (capacity, time windows, etc.) and returns optimized route sequences. It can be integrated into your own software via HTTP requests, giving you optimization power without building the algorithm yourself.

Can Google Maps do route optimization?

Google Maps can handle basic multi-stop routing for up to 10 stops, but it doesn't support vehicle capacity constraints, time windows, multi-driver splits, or real-time re-optimization — all of which are essential for professional delivery operations.

What algorithms are used for route optimization?

Modern route optimization uses a combination of techniques: nearest-neighbor heuristics for fast initial routes, 2-opt and 3-opt local search for refinement, and metaheuristics like genetic algorithms or simulated annealing for large-scale problems. AI and machine learning are increasingly used to predict traffic and improve estimates.

Can route optimization reduce carbon emissions?

Yes. By eliminating unnecessary mileage, optimized routes directly reduce fuel consumption. For electric fleets, shorter routes also extend battery life. Companies using Spoke Dispatch report up to 144 tonnes of CO₂ saved annually.

What's the difference between route optimization and GPS navigation?

GPS navigation finds the shortest path between two fixed points. Route optimization determines the best sequence of many stops across multiple vehicles, considering capacity, time windows, driver availability, and more — before navigation even begins.

How quickly can I get started?

Most teams are up and running within an hour. Import your stop list, add your drivers, click optimize — and your first routes are ready. No lengthy onboarding or IT integration required to start seeing value.
